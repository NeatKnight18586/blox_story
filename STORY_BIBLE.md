# STORY BIBLE — "In this town / world / kingdom…" (docs/V2_BRIEF.md §10)

Principles, not a template: the shape must change every episode. Apply inline, in one pass, whenever a new
script arrives (no subagents). Narration is always third person; characters may speak short quoted lines.

## The rule
- One sentence that makes an ordinary thing impossible, dangerous or counted. Frame 0 shows it.
- The rule is **visible like a game mechanic** (a tag, a counter, a stone, a height) — understandable with sound off.

## The hero
- Young, and the worst-placed person for the rule because of **one flaw**.
- That same flaw saves everyone at the end.

## The twist
- Fair: a loophole in the rule's exact wording, or a truth the adults missed.
- Re-watching shows the clue (planted early, shown more than once, never emphasised).
- A second, smaller surprise lands after the main twist.

## The ending
- Lands warm even when it costs something.
- The last line reframes the first; the final image rhymes with frame 0 (loop-friendly).

## Pacing for 2:02–2:50
- 400–520 words (the owner's voice reads ~200 words/min: 405 words ran 1:59.7). Sentences ≤ 12 words, ~5th-grade vocabulary, American English.
- A new question or mini-payoff every 20–25 s.
- Four movements of ~30 s, each ending on a turn.
- One laugh in the first 30 s, one in the last 30 s.
- Understandable with the sound off (captions always on, rule visible on screen).

## Safety
Family-safe: no gore, no on-screen death, no weapons used on people, no romance, no real brands.
Monsters are scared off, bonked, out-thought — never killed.

## Originality
- Reject rules already used in the niche: battery percentage, no frowning, never step on the ground, giant
  mouth, never go outside, luck number, Simon says, five-second floor, hide from the Seeker, crying turns you
  into your pet, photo day.
- Borrow the *engine* of a famous story, never its characters, names, imagery or beats.
- `SERIES_LOG.md`: one row per episode (setting, rule type, hook form, twist type, ending type, palette,
  landmark, ground plan). A new episode differs from the previous three in ≥ 5 of 8 columns and never
  repeats a landmark or ground plan.

## Council scorecard (1–5 each, one line of reasoning each)
All ≥ 4 to proceed; otherwise list exact line edits and stop. One revision round.
| Seat | Asks |
|---|---|
| Screenwriter | One-sentence rule? Fair twist? Does the flaw save the day? |
| Director | Is every sentence showable with our kit? A new image every 3 s? |
| 12-year-old US viewer | Clear without context? Shareable? Nothing boring or babyish? |
| Originality & safety | Not a niche repeat? Family-safe? |
| **Retention mentor (chair)** | Frame 0 works muted? Open question at 3, 20, 45, 70, 95 s? No dead stretch > 4 s? Ending loops? |

## Animatic checklist (run on the 1-fps sheets)
1. Frame 0 shows the rule as text and image.
2. No two adjacent cells look alike.
3. Hero or key object findable in under a second in every cell.
4. The twist cell is the biggest visual change in the episode.
5. The final cell rhymes with frame 0.

## Craft notes that carry between episodes
- The hero carries the episode's single accent colour (scarf, badge, hat) so they are found in any crowd.
- Name tags/counters are story devices: they pop when they change and say exactly what the rule says.
- Inserts of the clue get wider each time they appear (the audience should almost notice).
- Captions: 1–3 words, emphasis words in the accent yellow, never over a face (move high for that shot).

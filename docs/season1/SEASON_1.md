# Season 1: the 30-story slate, format and rules

Owner-approved creative source for every episode in the season. Claude Code executes this; it does not rewrite stories,
titles, rules or twists. A card that cannot be staged as written → say so in one paragraph and stop.

## 1. What separates thousands, millions and tens of millions

Read from the owner's study of four leading channels (their top-5 lists, view counts as the owner collected them):

| Tier | What those videos have | Example pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Thousands | The idea needs explaining. Frame 0 is ordinary. Looks like every other upload. | a rule you must hear to understand |
| Millions (5–10M) | One-sentence rule shown as a visible mechanic, an underdog with a defect, a crisis, the defect wins. | glowing UI on bodies, quirky custom material, survival-rule horror |
| Tens of millions (13–21M) | All of the above **plus** a premise you understand from one muted picture, built on primal forces at world scale, with a 3–5 word forbidden-command title. | two kingdoms of opposite elements, an ocean that retreats, a line you must not cross |
| Hundreds of millions | No single video in the study reaches it. It is a **channel total**: dozens of strong episodes plus long compilations. | library depth |

So the season is weighted toward world-scale, picture-first stories, and every episode must pass the **mute test**: frame 0
and the next three seconds explain the rule with no sound and no caption.

## 2. Steal like an artist: what we take, what we add

| From | We take the principle | We never take |
|---|---|---|
| Epic elemental channels | world-scale spectacle; drone establishing shot then a low heroic angle; command titles | their elements, kingdoms, plots |
| Rule/sci-fi channels | the rule as glowing UI attached to bodies; three rapid examples with hard cuts | their mechanics (battery, two lives, moving streets, glowing steps) |
| Quirky art channels | one custom material or creature per episode; tilt-up scale shots | drawings that come alive, paper worlds |
| Horror-lore channels | strict survival rules; slow push-ins; a loved one in doubt | their monsters and tests |

**Our own five signatures (every episode):**
1. **The Troupe.** The same five original actors play a new role in every world (A4 §G). Viewers learn their faces.
2. **Three clues.** Three visible clues to the twist are on screen before it happens. Rewatching pays off.
3. **AFK.** A hidden, faintly transparent, frozen player tagged `AFK` appears in exactly two shots of every episode. The finale is his story.
4. **A second surprise** after the reversal (why the rule existed, or what it cost). Length: [FORMAT v6](FORMAT_V6.md).
5. **The last line twists the rule or lands the final laugh or feeling** (never repeats the opening line), over a close-up or two-shot on the payoff ([FORMAT v6](FORMAT_V6.md)).

## 2b. Originality rule (checked before every script, and again by the owner)

A premise is the **rule + the picture + the twist**. Ours may share a genre with someone else's video; it may never share
two of those three. Already made by others, so never used here, not even reworded:
battery percentage over heads · two lives · streets that move · glowing footsteps · grandfather paradox · a copycat ·
drawings or scribbles that come alive · paper storm · cloned by a robot · many hands · a word limit · water and fire
kingdoms · the ocean pulling back · a line you must not cross · a stone that gives animal powers · people float when they
lie · tears turn to glass · no frowning · never touch the ground · a giant mouth · never go outside · a luck number ·
Simon says · hide from the Seeker · crying turns you into your pet · photo day · dream clothes · rubber sidewalk ·
everyone wears a bell · everyone lives forever · a voice in your head · insults come true · running turns you to gold ·
you can only say sorry · being unlucky is good · **a pet that may speak one sentence** (removed from this slate).
Famous films and books count too (no freeing the genie, no toys that wake up, no city in a bunker).

Before writing any script (§5b) Claude Code searches the web, when a search tool is available, for the card's Rule
sentence and title together with "roblox story", and reports anything close in two lines **before** the script. The
owner is the final check: `/season swap` replaces a card he has already seen elsewhere (A4 §I).

## 2c. Owner rule: no labels (from episode 1 on)

No name tags, no role tags, no "AFK" tag, no rarity or stat text over people or objects, no chat bubbles. Characters
are introduced by the narration and the camera; AFK is recognised by his grey, see-through, frozen look. Text appears
only where it physically exists in the world and the story needs it (a scoreboard, a clock, a note, a sign, a switch)
and in the captions. Every card's "Game echo" is shown through colour, light, shapes and motion, not on-screen UI.

## 3. Beat shape (length, word count, narration, end, final shot and hero-first: [FORMAT v6](FORMAT_V6.md); the target times below are legacy proportions)

| Beat | Target time | What happens | Camera | Must be on screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 Rule | 0:00–0:08 | "In this world/town, …" + the one rule, two sentences | `openHit`, `drone` over the spectacle, snap to hero face by 0:05 | the rule as a picture; hero face ≥ 20 % of frame height |
| B2 Expansion | 0:08–0:25 | three rapid, funny or eerie examples of life under the rule; first laugh | hard cuts on words, up to three `beat` shots, inserts | one new image per example |
| B3 Defect | 0:25–0:42 | the hero, the flaw, who laughs at it, what they want | medium orbit, eye level; `gamecam` once | clue 1 |
| B4 Crack | 0:42–1:00 | something does not add up; the hero bends the rule | slow push, over-shoulder | clue 2; AFK cameo 1 |
| B5 Crisis | 1:00–1:25 | a world-scale threat; the rule-followers fail | wide `crane`, then `heroLow` (low angle, FOV 60–70), `shake` on impacts | clue 3; deadline |
| B6 Low point | 1:25–1:35 | the hero alone; narration slows | static-drift close-up, 3–5 s hold allowed | face only |
| B7 Reversal | 1:35–1:50 | the defect solves it; biggest picture of the episode | `heroLow` → `orbit`; `flash` once | the largest visual change in the episode |
| B8 Second surprise | 1:50–2:05 | the truth about the rule | insert on the proof, then wide | the proof object |
| B9 Payoff | last line | the last line twists the rule or lands the final laugh or feeling (never the first line again) | close-up or two-shot on the payoff, never a wide | AFK cameo 2 |

Rules: sentences ≤ 12 words; ~5th-grade vocabulary; American English; no stretch > 4 s without new information; first-person
narration for the hero (D1); family-safe (peril resolves, no on-screen harm, no weapons used on people, no romance).

**Titles:** 3–6 words + one emoji. Either a forbidden command or the rule itself. No hashtags in the title.
**Pinned comment:** one either/or question about the hero's choice.

## 4. Lanes and rotation

`E` Epic (world-scale, primal) · `M` Mechanic (rule as UI) · `L` Lore (eerie survival rule) · `Q` Quirky (custom material or scale).
Block 1 (episodes 01–10) is ordered by the owner: game-genre worlds first. Never three episodes of the same lane in a
row. Never the same twist type twice within five episodes. After every five uploads,
`docs/season1/RESULTS.md` decides whether lane weights shift (§7).

## 5. Calendar: one episode per day, in card order

| Day | Work | Owner's part |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Sessions 8 and 9 (A4 build). Scripts 01 `egg`, 02 `ragdoll`, 11 `mountain` and 12 `mirror` already exist. | review the look, hair, troupe and captions; voice 01 |
| 1–30 | The daily loop below, cards 01 → 30 in order. | voice the next script; say approve, continue, or give notes |
| every 5th episode | Long compilation of the last five finals with chapters (assembled from existing masters, no re-render). | upload |

**Daily loop (`/season`, state kept in `docs/season1/STATE.md`):**
1. `animatic`: the voice for episode N is committed → align, world, cast, shots, gates, animatic. Stop.
2. Owner: "approve" or notes (one revision round).
3. `final`: 1440 final, QC, release, proxy, title and pinned comment. **In the same session, write the script and scene
   plan for episode N+1** (§5b), show the script in the report, and stop. The owner voices it for the next day.

A day slips only when a gate fails; nothing is skipped to keep the date, and the order never changes.
Time budget per episode: animatic session ≤ 1 session; final render + assemble ≤ 3 h wall. If that is exceeded two
episodes running, cut vegetation density and far-tier detail (never hero tier, never resolution) and record it.
Backlog (used by `/season swap`, then after 30), in this order: the spy who narrates the wrong person · the talking van
that only gives bad directions · the piggy-bank parade · the red string between strangers · doors to other yous · one
wish and you are forgotten · you may own one thing · kids are the bosses.

## 5b. How the remaining scripts are written (legacy guidance: the owner writes every script, decision D1 in `V4.md`)

Models: `stories/egg/script.txt`, `stories/ragdoll/script.txt`, `stories/mountain/script.txt`, `stories/mirror/script.txt`.
Match their rhythm and plainness. A script that already exists is never rewritten by the loop.
- First run the originality check in §2b and report it.
- B1 uses the card's **Rule** sentence(s); the ending never repeats the first line ([FORMAT v6](FORMAT_V6.md) rule 3).
- Everything on the card appears: the three expansion examples, the defect, the crisis, the reversal, the second surprise.
  Clues are shown, and at most one of the three is spoken. Nothing on the card is changed or "improved".
- Word count and narration per [FORMAT v6](FORMAT_V6.md) (first person, no cap). Sentences ≤ 12 words.
  One tense per script. ~5th-grade words. Characters' spoken lines: ≤ 6 words each, at most six per script, no quotation
  marks.
- One laugh inside B2, one inside B3–B4. A full stop of silence (a blank line) before the reversal line.
- No brand names, no real games' names, no on-screen harm, no romance.
- Then write `stories/<slug>/beats.md` in the same table form as the models, using §3's beats and cameras.
- Run `python3 scripts/v2/scriptcheck.py --story <slug>` (A4 §I) and the §10 council scorecard (all ≥ 4, one revision
  pass). Print the final script in the session report inside one code block so the owner can copy it.

## 6. The 30 cards

Card fields: **Rule** (spoken in B1) · **Frame 0** · **Hero / defect** · **Expansion** (three examples) · **Crisis → reversal**
· **Second surprise / last line** · **Clues** (three) · **Money shot** (the frame people screenshot) · **World**.

**Block 1 · episodes 01–10 · worlds that echo a Roblox game genre the audience already plays.** We take the genre's
picture (so a player recognises it in one second), never a game's name, map, UI art, characters or sounds.

### 01 · M · `egg` · Never Hold The Egg At Midnight 🥚
- **Rule:** In this town, everybody steals eggs. One rule: never hold one at midnight.
- **Frame 0:** night; a town of colour-coded bases, kids sprinting with glowing eggs, the clock tower at 11:59, a huge winged shadow crossing the moon.
- **Hero / defect:** Kit, 11, too honest to steal: score zero, returns eggs with a sorry note. Her brother Dax is number one.
- **Expansion:** bases lock for sixty seconds · the town-hall scoreboard, and the mayor plays in a ski mask · at 11:59 the whole town plays hot potato.
- **Crisis → reversal:** Dax holds one at midnight and is gone, one giant feather on his pillow; Kit steals her first egg and stands still in the square at midnight while the town yells to drop it; a dragon lifts her, gently, to a cloud nest where every missing kid has a hatched baby dragon.
- **Second surprise / last line:** eggs only hatch if held still through midnight, and a hundred years of stealing hatched none; the mother was never taking thieves, she was taking babysitters. / "And at midnight, everybody holds on."
- **Clues:** the town-hall weathervane is a dragon · claw marks on the clock-tower roof · eggs glow warm only in still hands (spoken).
- **Money shot:** a cloud nest the size of the town, full of kids with sleeping baby dragons. **World:** base town at night + cloudtop nest. Mother = `dragon.js` at 3×; hatchlings at 0.2×. Nobody is shown being taken; everyone comes home.
- **Game echo:** base-stealing games (bases, lock timers, a scoreboard). No game's name, map, UI art or characters.
- **Eggs:** nine original egg designs, one per themed base, with rarity glows (table in `beats.md`); Kit holds the Plain egg nobody wanted, and it hatches the only gold dragon.
- Script: `stories/egg/script.txt` · beats: `stories/egg/beats.md`.

### 02 · Q · `ragdoll` · Ragdoll Town 🪆
- **Rule:** In this town, nobody can stand up. Everyone is a ragdoll. Everyone except Stan.
- **Frame 0:** drone down a steep street of floppy people tumbling downhill, one launched overhead by a cannon; one kid standing perfectly straight.
- **Hero / defect:** Stan, 12, "Stiff Stan": the only person who can stand; cannot roll, cannonball or even slouch; wants to flop more than anything.
- **Expansion:** a cannon fires the grown-ups to work · stairs are slides and handshakes take three tries · everyone ends at the bottom of the hill, where a big net carries them home.
- **Crisis → reversal:** the net snaps; under it is a cliff; the whole town slides and floppy hands cannot grab; Stan does the one thing nobody else can and walks uphill, along a black cable, to a house-sized switch: RAGDOLL: ON. He pushes it and the town stands up.
- **Second surprise / last line:** nobody was born floppy, somebody left a setting on; standing turns out to be boring, so the town votes it back on for weekends and Stan finally flops. / "Not on weekends, anyway."
- **Clues:** a glowing cog icon on the summit from frame 0 · every doorknob and coat hook sits at standing height · the cable running uphill (spoken).
- **Money shot:** one block-kid under a giant floating toggle switch above the clouds. **World:** steep hill town, catch-net, cliff, summit. Authored flop poses (`flop`, `tumble`, `launched`, `slide`, `plank`), no physics engine.
- **Game echo:** ragdoll-physics playgrounds and the settings menu. Original panel art.
- Script: `stories/ragdoll/script.txt` · beats: `stories/ragdoll/beats.md`.

### 03 · L · `eight` · It Comes At Eight 🔔
- **Rule:** In this town, a monster comes every night at eight. It's always one of us.
- **Frame 0:** clock tower at 7:59; shutters slamming down a street; hero's face in a doorway.
- **Hero / defect:** Eli, 12, slowest runner, "always caught first", remembers nothing by morning.
- **Expansion:** five locks on every door · dinner at five · hiding drills at school.
- **Crisis → reversal:** he sets a camera to learn who it is; the footage shows the monster leaving *his* house with his untied shoelace.
- **Second surprise / last line:** it only comes when he is alone; a friend holds his hand at 7:59 and the bell rings on an empty street. / "It just didn't want to be alone."
- **Clues:** mud on his shoes each morning · neighbours never meet his eyes · the monster's untied lace.
- **Money shot:** two kids holding hands under a clock striking eight. **World:** the suburb at dusk, fog, single streetlights. "Caught" = frozen until morning, never harm.
- **Game echo:** hide-and-survive games (a round timer, one hunter, many hiders). No game's name, map, UI art or characters.

### 04 · M · `duel` · Every Argument Is A Duel 🎯
- **Rule:** In this city, every argument is settled by a duel. Winner takes all.
- **Frame 0:** a rooftop arena over a neon city; two kids back to back holding splat balls, a giant "VS" card between them, rank badges over every head in the crowd.
- **Hero / defect:** Remy, 12, the worst aim in the city: 0 wins, 911 losses. She laughs every time she loses, so she is the only one who still thinks duels are fun.
- **Expansion:** siblings duel for the front seat · teachers duel students over homework · the mayor is simply whoever is ranked number one.
- **Crisis → reversal:** Rex, number one, never smiles; he duels the whole city and wins everyone's Saturdays, then challenges for recess, forever; the best players all lose. Remy, ranked last, challenges him and picks the game: first one to laugh loses. Her jokes fail; she throws one splat ball as a gag, misses by a mile, and it bounces off the scoreboard, the mascot and a flagpole back into her own face. Rex laughs so hard he falls off the podium.
- **Second surprise / last line:** the banner on the arena gate falls: the sign under it always said CHALLENGER PICKS THE GAME. Duels were invented to end fights with play; next day the city duels by hopscotch, thumb war and pie. / "Yesterday's was a pillow fight."
- **Clues:** the gate sign half covered by a banner ("CHALLENGER PICKS THE G…") · the oldest mural shows duels with jump ropes and spoons · a dusty joke book in Rex's trophy case.
- **Money shot:** a packed stadium watching two kids stare at each other, trying not to laugh. **World:** downtown rooftops + arena. Soft splat balls only, thrown at targets and vests; nothing that looks like a weapon.
- **Game echo:** 1-v-1 arena duel games (VS cards, ranks, best-of rounds). No game's name, map, UI art or characters.

### 09 · M · `dice` · The Dice Above Your Head 🎲
- **Rule:** In this town, a dice rolls over your head every morning. It decides your day.

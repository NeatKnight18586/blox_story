## Story lock / interpretation

**Core theme:** Leo plans against chance; Mayor Pip has trapped his town to avoid facing a changed family. The rigged die wobbles because Pip triggers a brass paddle whenever a six would land. Leo takes out the mechanism's pin and leaves the decision with Pip. The next five mornings are fair non-six rolls. On the sixth morning six occurs naturally and opens the **existing town gate** (not a magical die portal), allowing Pip to reunite with his older mother. The preserved childhood chair provides the payoff. Leo closes his notebook without planning tomorrow.

**Visual-continuity issue:** Leo is secured to a clocktower anchor, then reaches the under-plinth hatch. Stage the tether as long enough to traverse the square; show it being re-clipped to a fixed anchor near the hatch before he descends. The visible action can be conveyed with an insert; no full rope dynamics required. If the voice narration claims he is literally tied to a fixed tower at all times, make the path and rope length visually plausible.

**Fairness issue:** Removing the paddle pin does **not** force six on the sixth sunrise. The five failures and sixth success are chance, not a secretly deterministic rule. Pip chooses not to reinstall the pin.

### Four editorial blocks

1. **0:00–0:29.5: Hook + rules + suspicion.** The die visibly causes levitation immediately; Leo is secured by a tether; rule 1, rule 2, and the absent six are shown through extremely brief inserts.
2. **0:29.5–1:14.1: Investigation + the choice.** Leo notices the repeated unnatural wobble, descends into the small service chamber, finds Pip manipulating the paddle, and leaves the pin with him.
3. **1:14.1–1:36.6: Five mornings + six.** Reuse plaza shots and changed die faces; Pip's repeated decision is the emotional event, not an additional action spectacle.
4. **1:36.6–1:57.1: Gate + reunion + closing book.** Open an existing-looking gate, move to a minimal doorway/home set, focus on recognition, chair, and Leo's last gesture.

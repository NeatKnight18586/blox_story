In this world, the mountains are sleeping giants.
And there's only one rule. Never wake them.

So the whole valley lives in whispers.
Church bells are wrapped in wool. Dogs wear tiny socks.
Babies learn to cry into pillows.
Even the thunder gets shushed.
The school has no bell. Just a man who waves.
Because the last time a giant woke up, it rolled over. And a whole town was gone.

Then there's Bo.
Bo is eleven. And Bo is loud.
Loud laugh. Loud sneeze. Loud whisper.
His own echo once asked him to stop.
Once, he said good morning, and three windows cracked.
So Bo lives in the last house in the valley. The one stuffed with pillows.
Every night, he looks up at the mountain. And says nothing.

But lately, Bo noticed something strange.
The mountain's shadow used to end at the river. Now it ends at the bridge.
He told everyone. In his quietest voice.
Nobody listened. Mountains don't move.
Only his little sister believed him. She was six. She believed everything.

Then one night, the ground started shaking.
The giant wasn't waking up. It was worse.
It was sleepwalking.
One step flattened the forest. The next one flattened the old mill.
The third step was headed straight for the village.

The elders ran outside. And they did the only thing they knew.
They whispered. Please. Stop.
The giant didn't hear a thing.

Bo stood in the road. His little sister was still inside the school.
All his life, people told him the same four words. Bo, keep it down.
He took the deepest breath of his life.
And he screamed.

Windows shattered. Every bird in the valley took off at once. The wool blew off every bell.
And high in the clouds, one giant eye opened.
The foot stopped. Right above the school.
The whole valley froze. This was it. The giant was awake.

The giant looked down. At the broken trees. The tiny houses. The tiny, loud boy.
And very, very slowly, it stepped back.
Then it sat down. And it stayed awake. And nothing bad happened at all.
For the first time in a hundred years, somebody rang a bell.

That's when they dug up the old stone sign. Half of it had been buried for a hundred years.
Everyone knew the top half. Never wake the giant.
Nobody had ever read the bottom half.
Unless it walks.

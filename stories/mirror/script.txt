In this town, every mirror shows tomorrow.
This morning, Max looked in his. He wasn't there.

Every mirror here runs one day ahead.
See a bruise? Wear a helmet. See a smile? Good day coming.
Everybody checks. Before school. Before work. Before a big test.
Mrs. Park saw herself holding a trophy. So she finally entered the pie contest.
But nobody had ever seen nothing.
Max waved. No Max. Just his room. And his bed, perfectly made.
Max had never made his bed in his life.

Max was twelve. And he was a later kid.
Say sorry? Later. Try out for the team? Later.
Tell Dad who broke the window? Way later.
Now there was no later. He had one day.

So Max made a list.
One. Cake for breakfast. The whole cake.
Two. He told his little sister, you can have my room. She had wanted it forever.
Three. He walked up to Brick, the biggest kid in school. And said, you're not scary. You're just loud.
Brick cried. Nobody saw that coming. Not even the mirrors.
Four. He tried out for the team. He was terrible. He did not care.
The coach wrote something down. Max didn't look.
Five. He told Dad about the window.
Dad already knew. He saw it in the mirror a week ago. He was just waiting.
Six. He hugged his mom for way too long. She let him.

By sunset, the list was done.
It was the best day of his life. And that made it so much worse.
That night, Max sat in front of his mirror. Still empty.
I finally like my life, he whispered. And tomorrow I'm not in it.
Then his sister walked in, dragging her pillow. You promised.
So Max took his blanket and slept in her tiny room.
He stared at the ceiling. And he waited for tomorrow.

Tomorrow came.
Max opened his eyes. He was still here.
He checked his hands. His feet. His face. All there.
He ran down the hall and threw open his old door.
The room was empty. The bed was perfectly made.
His sister wakes up at six. And makes her bed. For fun.
And Max finally understood.
The mirror never showed a world without him.
It showed his sister's room.
He wasn't in it, because he gave it away.

Max laughed so hard he fell over.
Then he checked the hallway mirror.
Tomorrow, he had mud on his face. And a team jersey.
He grinned. Worth it.

In this town, every mirror shows tomorrow.
They just never tell you why.

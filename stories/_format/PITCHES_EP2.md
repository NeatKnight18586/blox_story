# Episode 2 pitches (scored on the format bible rubric)

Scores: Hook image · Rule clarity · Gag density · Twist · Payoff · Set reuse · Novelty (each 1–5).

## A. Shortwick — "In this town, every lie makes you shorter." — 33/35
- **Hook image:** a knee-high mayor giving a speech from a stack of books, towering over nobody.
- **Demonstrations:** a fisherman brags about his catch and drops an inch mid-sentence; a boy says "I already did my homework" and his hat falls over his eyes; the used-cart salesman is the size of a shoe.
- **The kid:** Wren, the tallest girl in Shortwick. She has never told a lie, and the town treats her height as showing off.
- **Escalation:** Wren notices the kindest person in town, old Nan Tilly, is barely ankle-high, and everyone whispers she must be the worst liar alive. Wren sets out to prove it. Midpoint: she catches Nan mid-lie, telling a frightened child the thunder is "just the sky clearing its throat".
- **Twist:** Wren's little brother is sick and asks if he'll be all right. She says yes. She shrinks. Nan's size was never dishonesty; it was a lifetime of comfort given.
- **Ending:** Wren, a head shorter, sits beside tiny Nan on a doorstep. "In Shortwick, the smallest people are the ones who loved the most." 
- **Cost:** very low. Scaling characters is one number in our rig; the whole town is the existing kit plus one bedroom.
- **Scores:** 5 · 5 · 5 · 5 · 5 · 5 · 3

## B. Milo's Weather — "In this town, the weather is one boy's mood." — 31/35
- **Hook image:** a boy stubs his toe and lightning splits the bakery sign.
- **Demonstrations:** he laughs and the sun snaps on; he's bored and fog rolls in; he sneezes and it hails for a second.
- **The kid:** Milo, ten. The whole town performs for him all day, juggling and handing him cake, to keep the sun out.
- **Escalation:** the fountain is dry, the fields are yellow, and nobody says why. Midpoint: Milo overhears the grown-ups scheduling his happiness in shifts. The sky goes flat grey: he feels nothing.
- **Twist:** a girl who refuses to perform tells him the truth: it hasn't rained in ten years because nobody has let him be sad. He finally cries. It rains. The town needed the one feeling it spent a decade preventing.
- **Ending:** people dancing in the rain, the fountain filling; Milo smiling *in* the rain. "They'd kept him happy for ten years. All the town ever needed was to let him be sad."
- **Cost:** medium. Needs rain, fog, lightning and sky presets (reusable in later episodes).
- **Scores:** 5 · 5 · 4 · 5 · 5 · 3 · 4

## C. Wrong Door — "In this town, every door opens somewhere else." — 28/35
- **Hook image:** a man steps through his bathroom door and falls into the bakery's flour bin.
- **Demonstrations:** school door → the mayor's bathtub; fridge door → the clock tower ledge; a kid uses a cupboard to skip the queue.
- **The kid:** Bram, who just wants to get home for dinner and keeps arriving everywhere else.
- **Escalation:** each wrong room has someone who needs a small thing: a jar opened, a cat caught, a letter read. Midpoint: he realises the doors are not random.
- **Twist:** doors take you where you're needed, not where you want to go. His last door opens on an old man alone with a birthday cake — the grandfather he's never met.
- **Ending:** Bram's family comes through the same door behind him. "He never did find the way home. The door did."
- **Cost:** high. Many small interiors (but they become a reusable interior kit).
- **Scores:** 5 · 4 · 5 · 4 · 4 · 2 · 4

## D. The Shadow That Left — "In this town, your shadow does what you really want." — 27/35
- **Hook image:** a shopkeeper smiles at a customer while his shadow strangles the customer's shadow.
- **Demonstrations:** a boy says "I'm not hungry" as his shadow steals a pie; two rivals shake hands while their shadows hug; a teacher's shadow naps mid-lesson.
- **The kid:** Odile, the only person in town with no shadow at all.
- **Escalation:** everyone assumes she wants nothing. She follows where other shadows point at dusk. Midpoint: footprints of a shadow leading out of town.
- **Twist:** her shadow left years ago to follow her father down the road; it has been standing at the edge of town ever since, still waving.
- **Ending:** she stands beside it and waves too. It steps back onto her feet. "It hadn't left her. It had just been waiting where she couldn't bear to go."
- **Cost:** medium-high. Independent shadows need a new flat-silhouette technique.
- **Scores:** 5 · 4 · 4 · 4 · 4 · 2 · 4

## E. A Loaf Costs a Memory — "In this town, people pay with memories." — 26/35
- **Hook image:** a man at the bakery pulls a glowing photograph out of his head and hands it over for bread.
- **Demonstrations:** a rich woman's house is wallpapered in other people's birthdays; a man can't remember his dog's name but owns three hats; the price board reads "1 loaf = 1 summer".
- **The kid:** Tam, who has been selling memories to feed his little sister and is running low.
- **Escalation:** he sells smaller and smaller moments. Midpoint: he no longer recognises his own front door.
- **Twist:** his sister has been secretly working to buy every one of them back; she has a box of glowing photos under her bed.
- **Ending:** she hands him the box. He remembers her name last. "He'd sold everything he had for her. She'd spent everything she earned on him."
- **Cost:** medium. Reuses our photo renderer; needs a glow effect and two interiors.
- **Scores:** 4 · 4 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 3 · 3

## Decision
Owner picked **A (Shortwick)**, with a new cast every episode (no recurring Leo). Script: `stories/shortwick/SCRIPT.md`.

## Recommendation
**A (Shortwick)** first: the strongest hook image, the clearest rule, and almost free to build, so the effort goes into camera, acting and pacing — the things we're upgrading. **B (Milo's Weather)** second: its weather toolkit pays off across the series.

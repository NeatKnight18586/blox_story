In this town, nobody can stand up.
Everyone is a ragdoll. Everyone except Stan.

People here don't walk. They flop.
They roll to school. They tumble to work.
A cannon fires the grown-ups to their jobs. Most of them land in their chairs.
Stairs are slides. Every sidewalk is a ramp.
A handshake takes three tries and one apology.
And the town is built on a hill. So everybody ends up at the bottom.
Every night, a big net scoops them up and carries them home.

Then there's Stan.
Stan is twelve. And Stan stands.
Straight up. On two feet. Like a lamp.
The other kids call him Stiff Stan.
He can't cannonball. He can't roll. He can't even slouch.
Every recess, the whole class tumbles down the hill laughing.
Stan walks down behind them. Carrying everyone's shoes.
More than anything, he wants to flop.
He practices at night. He just tips over. Like a board.

But standing has one good thing. Stan can see far.
And from up there, he noticed something strange.
A thick black cable runs up the hill. All the way to the top.
Nobody has ever been to the top. You can't roll uphill.

Then one night, the big net snapped.
And under the net, there was no more hill. Just a cliff.
The whole town started sliding. Beds. Cars. The mayor. One very calm cow.
They grabbed for each other. Floppy hands don't grab.
Somebody yelled. Do something, Stan!
Stan looked down at the cliff. Then up at the cable.
For once, nobody needed him to flop.

So Stan did the one thing nobody else could do.
He walked uphill.
One step. Then another. Then he ran.
At the very top, there was a switch. As big as a house.
It had one word on it. Ragdoll. And under that, one more. On.
Stan pushed it with his whole body.
Click.
All over the hill, people stopped sliding.
And one by one, for the first time ever, they stood up.

Nobody was born floppy. Somebody left a setting on.
The town stood around for a whole week.
They waved. They shook hands on the first try.
And they were so bored.
So they voted. They turned it back on. Just for weekends.
That Saturday, Stan stood at the top of the hill.
The whole town chanted his name.
He took a deep breath. He stopped trying. And he flopped.
It was the best fall of his life.
He taught the town to stand. The town taught him to fall.

In this town, nobody can stand up.
Not on weekends, anyway.

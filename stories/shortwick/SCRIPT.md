# Shortwick — narration script (Episode 2)

**Logline:** In this town, every lie makes you shorter.
**Cast (new for this episode):** Wren (the tallest girl in town, never lied), Bo (her little brother), Nan Tilly (tiny, the kindest woman in town), the Mayor, the Fisherman, the cart salesman, Wren's teacher, Mr. Pell, a scared little boy, townsfolk.
**Length:** 402 words ≈ 2:08 at the Dice Town pace (1.25× narration, ~3.14 words/s).

## Recording notes
- Same voice and speed as Dice Town (ElevenLabs Adam, then 1.25×).
- Read it straight through as one file. Don't trim pauses: the edit is cut to your words automatically.
- The `[beat]` lines are for the picture only. Don't read them.
- Quoted lines are still the narrator, just a touch softer.

---

[beat — cold open, 0–3 s]
In this town, every lie makes you shorter.

[beat — three demonstrations, 3–20 s]
The mayor promised to fix the roads. Twice. Now he gives his speeches standing on a stack of books.

The fisherman swore his catch was this big... and lost an inch before he finished the sentence.

And the man who sells used carts? He's about the size of a shoe.

[beat — the kid, 20–40 s]
Then there was Wren.

Wren had never told a single lie. Not one. And in Shortwick, that made her the tallest girl in town.

People called it showing off. Kids made her sit in the back row. Even her teacher stood on a chair to talk to her.

[beat — re-hook]
But Wren noticed something strange.

The kindest woman in Shortwick, old Nan Tilly, was barely as tall as a teacup.

Everyone whispered that she must be the biggest liar who ever lived.

[beat — investigation, 40–60 s]
Wren didn't believe it. So she decided to find out.

She followed Nan everywhere.

Nan fed the stray cats. Nan fixed the baker's broken sign. Nan carried groceries up the hill for old Mr. Pell, which, for Nan, took most of the afternoon.

No lies. Not one.

[beat — midpoint, ~60 s]
Then the storm came.

Thunder cracked over the rooftops, and a little boy hid under Nan's porch, shaking.

Nan knelt beside him and smiled.

"That's just the sky clearing its throat," she said.

And Wren watched her shrink. Just a little.

[beat — false ending, 75–90 s]
So it was true. Nan was a liar.

Wren ran home, feeling silly for ever believing in her.

[beat — stakes and choice, 90–110 s]
That night, her little brother Bo had a fever.

He looked up at her from his bed, scared.

"Am I going to be okay?"

Wren didn't know.

She opened her mouth to tell the truth...

and instead, she heard herself say:

"Of course you are."

[beat — twist, 110–125 s]
Her sleeves slid past her fingers. Her shoes went loose.

She had shrunk.

And suddenly, Wren understood.

Nan Tilly had never lied to cheat anyone. She had spent her whole life telling scared people that everything would be fine.

Every inch she'd lost, she'd given away.

[beat — payoff, 125–132 s]
The next morning, Bo's fever broke.

Wren walked to Nan's house, a little shorter than yesterday, and sat beside her on the step. For the first time, they were eye to eye.

"Did it hurt?" Wren asked.

Nan laughed. "Only the first time."

[beat — button, last 5–8 s]
In Shortwick, everyone thinks the tallest people are the most honest.

But the smallest people in town are the ones who loved the most.
